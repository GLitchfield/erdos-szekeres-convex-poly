# E3 singleton-root rank/downset gate

## Status and question

This is bounded computational evidence, not a completed mathematical result.
It tests the correlation-preserving redirect selected after the maxima-profile
collision: can the classical consecutive-chain pair ranks, or their vertex
downsets, serve as a deterministic state for adjoining a singleton lower root?

The scope is fixed at `k = 6`, `n = 9`, lower child `{1}`, and upper child
`{0,2,...,8}`. Every distinct recursively decomposable coloring of the upper
child is enumerated. The resulting abstract triple colorings are signotopes,
but this experiment does not provide point coordinates or a certificate of
straight-line sweep realizability. Its negative conclusions therefore apply
to the abstract decomposable transition under test, not to every conceivable
transition that can exploit additional geometric information.

## States under test

For each ordered pair `u < v`, its cup and cap ranks are one less than the
longest same-turn consecutive chain ending at `(u,v)`, clipped at `k-2 = 4`.
The initial pair has both ranks one. A predecessor pair `(w,u)` extends to
`(u,v)` exactly in the coordinate matching the turn of `(w,u,v)`.

The downset at `v` is the union, over every `u < v`, of the lower-left
rectangle generated by the two ranks at `(u,v)`. It is encoded as a sixteen-
bit mask. Three increasingly weak claims are tested:

1. the complete ordered word of child vertex downsets determines the complete
   ordered word after adjoining `{1}`;
2. the complete ordered child pair-rank matrix determines the complete parent
   pair-rank matrix;
3. the downset injection can be counted using only pairwise incompatibility of
   raw downset values, with clique number at most `2^(k-2) = 16`.

Only parents with no strong six-set enter these transition tables.

## Producer, certificate, and independent checker

`scripts/analyze_decomposable_rank_profiles.py` performs the bottom-up coloring
enumeration, retains one explicit decomposition tree per coloring, computes
both state transitions, and constructs the observed parent-downset
compatibility graph. It emits the compact certificate
`data/derived/e3-rank-k6-n9-singleton.json`.

`scripts/check_decomposable_rank_profiles.py` imports no producer code. It
regenerates the coloring set by a separately written recurrence, recomputes
every open state and canonical digest, descends all four explicit collision
trees, and checks the compatibility graph. For the maximum clique claim it
uses an independent exact reduction: maximum clique in the 34-vertex graph is
34 minus minimum vertex cover in its complement, and a bounded branching
search finds the latter exactly.

The certificate labels commit `89ba0c9` as its generation base rather than
claiming that a not-yet-written output already existed at that revision. It
also records the exact SHA-256 of its producer, which the checker verifies.
The specification-first unit test independently enumerates every consecutive
chain for every triple coloring through five vertices and clips one through
five. Those direct maxima and rectangles agree with the producer's dynamic
program in every case. This closes the shared-recursion semantic check without
using signotope or realizability assumptions.

The producer took 19.4 seconds and 353 MiB peak RSS. The independent replay
took 21.4 seconds and 311 MiB peak RSS. Both are far below the declared
30-minute, 16-GiB bound.

## Exact results

Both programs agree on:

- 186,888 distinct upper-child colorings;
- 85,797 parents without a strong six-set;
- 15,216 distinct child downset words and 9,535 observations where equal
  child words produce unequal parent words;
- 55,537 distinct child pair-rank matrices and 481 observations where equal
  child matrices produce unequal parent matrices;
- zero failures of vertex-to-downset injectivity inside any open parent;
- 34 observed raw parent downsets and 505 observed compatibility edges;
- an exact maximum compatibility clique of size 27.

The first downset-word collision differs first at parent vertex index seven,
where the masks are 307 and 819. The first full-matrix collision differs first
at pair coordinate 32, where the ranks are `(4,2)` and `(3,2)`. Each side is
recorded by its upper coloring, parent coloring, and an explicit decomposition
tree. The maximum clique is also listed explicitly in the certificate.

## Interpretation

The ordered downset word is not a compositionally complete singleton-root
state. Even retaining every child pair rank is not sufficient: the parent
rank of a pair can depend on predecessor correlations that the child matrix
does not determine once the new vertex is inserted into the sweep order.

The raw downsets remain injective in every covered open parent, so this census
does not refute the classical global injection itself. It does rule out the
proposed pairwise counting shortcut: the observed compatibility graph already
has a 27-clique, exceeding 16. A surviving rank route must therefore use a
higher-order restriction, a different canonical label, or realizability data;
it cannot use only the deterministic child states or pairwise raw-downset
incompatibility tested here.

No uniform replacement route is selected by this negative gate. Before more
composition machinery is added in Lean, the next candidate must state exactly
which correlations it retains, why its state count depends only on `k`, and
whether its transition is claimed for abstract decomposable colorings or only
for sweep-realizable ones.

## Lean consequence and advisory review

`ErdosSzekeres/RankProfile.lean` now defines the specification directly in
terms of `CupCap.IsTurnChain`, rather than importing the Python recurrence into
the theorem statement. Its first compiling lemmas establish subset
monotonicity, rank-threshold antitonicity, the base rank-one pair, and
lower-rectangle closure. Their axiom audit contains only `propext` and
`Quot.sound`. This semantic layer is unconditional for arbitrary triple
colorings; the finite collision itself remains to be encoded and proved in
Lean.

The first Sol audit at packet SHA-256
`04b7300160c56e5c0e06dc4617b2af96812eb03edf6d2f1cd402b8848b48da3c`
returned `partially_supported` and agreed with all three bounded rejections.
Its two major objections were the misleading pre-generation revision label
and the shared dynamic-programming semantics. Both were disposed by the
source-bound certificate and exhaustive direct-chain test described above.

A clean corrected packet at commit `bbafd69` has SHA-256
`b52e93af2a71e4cb1da99de9429a52ff752a86d3871a1a2a3343994384042d8a`.
The combined corrected review exhausted its 30-minute provider stage without
writing a result, and a separate 15-minute Fable attempt also returned zero
bytes. No process remained. This is provider unavailability, not reviewer
agreement or disagreement; no Fable conclusion is claimed.
