# B. Extremal set theory and the Boolean lattice (survey, 2026-10-04)

All tests are diagnostic Python or SAT runs, not Lean. Scripts and outputs are in `b_tests/` (`o_tests_a.txt`, `o_t11_*.txt`).
The corpus is `runs/claude-bandrule-20261003/corpus67_tight.pkl`: 522 tight sets, 345 of them in the BDL regime.

## 0. Two framing facts

**F1. Where B_{k-2} already sits.** The Moshkovitz–Shapira (MS) pigeonhole proof of cups-vs-caps works as follows.
- Give point v the down-set D_v generated by the pairs (longest cup ending at uv, longest cap ending at uv), over u < v.
- v ↦ D_v is injective into the down-sets of a (u−2)×(a−2) box.
- In a tight set, every BDL band X_j is an extremal cups-caps set of size C(k−2,j). So MS inside a band is a bijection onto the j-subsets of [k−2].
- Hence **BDL is equivalent to an injection X → window(B_{k−2}) whose levels are bands**.
- Iterating PSL k−2 times is the same as a prefix-coded injection: each step R/Bl is one coordinate.

What does not carry the Boolean structure:
- **Tree addresses.** The reduced trees of tight sets have depth up to n−1 (caterpillars), for example 13 at (5,5,6) with k−2 = 4 (t3).
- **The global MS profile.** It is injective on all 522 sets (t1). But cutting the profile down to its first or last k−2 boundary steps, which leaves exactly T values, is not injective: 0/30 at (6,6,6), 0/30 at (5,6,6), and 855 and 900 of 2135 random BDL-regime sets (t5).
- **The image of the MS map.** It has no rank-window or ideal structure. Its longest inclusion chain is 7–13 at (6,6,6), against 5 bands (t2, t4).

**F2. A root split cuts the window at a level.** Take X = A << B with p = cup A and q = cap B.
- For k−a < p < u−1 we have **T(a,p+1,k) + T(k−p,u,k) = T(a,u,k)**: A uses levels [k−a, p−1] and B uses levels [p, u−2]. Checked for all k ≤ 11; it fails exactly outside this range.
- With Lemma DS (q ≤ k−1−p), induction on |X| therefore proves Theorem 8 at every root split with k−a < p < u−1.
- The exceptions are p ≤ k−a and p ≥ u−1. In the corpus every exception is the tight case **p = k−a** (or the mirror q = k−u).
- At p = k−a, A's share of the window is empty, yet A can hold C(k−3,p−1) points. That is exactly **the star of one element inside level p of B_{k−2}**, since C(k−2,p) = C(k−3,p) + C(k−3,p−1).
- Corpus check (t8):
  - 247 of 345 tight root splits are exact level cuts.
  - All 98 others are this degenerate cut: 57 have p = k−a and 41 have q = k−u.
- **In Boolean terms, MIXED ROOT says: A must displace a star of level p.**

## 1. Shifting / compression

- **Idea.** EKR (1961) and Frankl's survey (1987) use shifts S_ij(F), replacing j by i whenever the result is new. A shift preserves the property, strictly lowers Σ elements, and ends at a shifted family that is easy to analyse. The down-shift proofs of Sauer–Shelah (Frankl 1983; Alon 1983) are the same pattern: the family of shattered sets can only shrink.
- **Application.** One needs an order on positions such that moving a point "down" never increases cup, cap or strong, applied to all points at once. The terminal form would be a canonical (ES-like) configuration.
- **Test (t7).** Take x-adjacent transpositions across an LCA, 11,203 of them on 1,500 random sets. Each direction raises cup, cap or strong in about 21% of cases; strong rises 470 and 462 times. Root-level swaps never change strong (4,171 swaps, which confirms Lemma S). So deeper swaps change strong through the subtrees' cup and cap.
- **Verdict: low.** No elementwise order is monotone, and the configuration-level version is the failed rectification.

## 2. Kruskal–Katona and cascades

- **Idea.** Kruskal (1963) and Katona (1968): colex segments minimise shadows. Clements–Lindström (1969) extend this to products of chains.
- **Application.** A size-sensitive Theorem 8 via cascades would need a shadow relation between bands, and BDL bands have none.
- **Test.** None run.
- **Verdict: low.**

## 3. LYM and Bollobás-type inequalities

- **Idea.** Lubell (1966): Σ 1/C(n,|F|) ≤ 1 for an antichain. Erdős (1945): a family with no (r+1)-chain has at most the r largest levels. Bollobás (1965) proves the analogous inequality for set pairs.
- **Application.** The right normalisation is Σ_x 1/C(k−2, ρ(x)) ≤ #bands, with ρ intrinsic.
  - That inequality alone does not give T. It also needs the level capacities |ρ⁻¹(j)| ≤ C(k−2,j), which come free in B_n.
  - Because the window is full, the capacities alone already give T. So LYM adds nothing beyond "intrinsic BDL".
- **Test (t6).** Six natural ranks were tried: longest cup or cap ending at, starting at, or passing through x. They satisfy the capacities on only 98–109 of 345 tight BDL-regime sets, and 0 of 345 for the "through" ranks.
- **Verdict: low.** This is consistent with "tight sets have no forced choices".

## 4. Symmetric chain decompositions and chain-to-chain maps

- **Idea.**
  - de Bruijn–Tengbergen–Kruyswijk (1951) build an SCD inductively, B_n = B_{n−1}×{0,1}. Each chain c_1 ⊂ … ⊂ c_t yields (c_1, …, c_t, c_t+n) and (c_1+n, …, c_{t−1}+n): the top element is lifted and the rest shift.
  - Greene–Kleitman (1976) bracketing is a canonical rule with unbounded lookahead.
- **Application.** By F2, the lock is the BTK step at level p: A takes the star of level p, and the displaced points must move up. This is a template for keep-or-lift, possibly with a bracketing rule for the "which ones" choice.
- **Test.**
  - **Star form (t10).** Ask for a BDL partition of X in which B's points in band p have cap ≤ k−2−p, i.e. B keeps only the C(k−3,p) half. This holds at **57/57** small tight borrowing splits. In 54 of them |A| = C(k−3,p−1).
  - **Hard lock sets (t11).** On `claude-configH-20261004/cex40.txt` (n = 40, k = 8, p = 2), with nested caps A:
    - all 45 placements with a 2-point A have BDL, KEEP and STAR;
    - the 3 keep-failing placements tested with a 4-point A have **BDL but no star form**.
  - So a simple one-band star swap is not the mechanism. The displacement has to propagate across bands, which is what SCD chains encode.
- **Caveat.** An earlier run found that repair paths need multi-band swap "stars", not chains. SCD chains differ because they are global, not repair paths.
- **Verdict: medium.**

## 5. Normalized matching and Sperner theory

- **Idea.** Kleitman (1974): for ranked posets, LYM ⇔ normalized matching ⇔ a regular chain cover. Existence follows from flows. Stanley (1980) and Proctor (1982) show that the Young lattices L(m,n) are strongly Sperner.
- **Application and test.**
  - A k-Sperner bound on the MS images in L(α−2, β−2) is impossible: at (6,6,6) the 5 largest ranks sum to 14 < 16 = T, and the images do contain long chains (t4).
  - Matchings between bands are already ruled out: Hall's condition is insufficient (X14) and the polytope is not integral.
- **Verdict: low.**

## 6. Harper and Lindström-type isoperimetry

- **Idea.** Harper (1966): Hamming balls minimise vertex boundary. Kleitman (1966): the diameter theorem. Bollobás–Leader (1991): compressions on grids.
- **Application.** The window is Ball(∅, u−2) ∩ Ball([k−2], a−2) in Q_{k−2}. Using this needs an embedding of X in the cube, which is again the missing injection.
- **Test.** None run.
- **Verdict: low.**

## 7. Theorems whose answer is a window of binomials

- **Sauer–Shelah (1972).** A family with VC-dimension below d has at most Φ_{d−1}(m) = Σ_{i<d} C(m,i) sets. **Our a = k column is exactly this: T(k,u,k) = Φ_{u−2}(k−2), and PSL is exactly the Sauer–Shelah recursion.**
  - Pajor's proof (book, 1985; not checked) gives |sh F| ≥ |F|.
  - Anstee–Rónyai–Sali (2002): exactly |F| sets are order-shattered. These are the standard monomials of F's vanishing ideal, so the proof is a linear-algebra statement.
- **Alon–Babai–Suzuki conjecture (1991), proved by Hwang–Kim (2015).** For L-intersecting families with sizes in K, the bound is Σ_{i=s−r+1}^{s} C(n,i), a window of r = |K| levels. Ours is a window of r = #bands levels ending at u−2. The proof uses multilinear polynomials plus the swallowing trick, with no partition.
- **Application.** Find vectors f_x in V(a,u,k) = span{x_S : k−a ≤ |S| ≤ u−2} (dimension T) that satisfy a triangular criterion.
  - V splits as V(a−1,u,k−1) ⊕ x_{k−2}·V(a,u−1,k−1), which mirrors PSL. But linear independence does not need the parts to be disjoint.
  - MS's ordered relation (D_w ⊄ D_v for v < w) is the template.
  - This is the only tool here that bounds |X| without constructing a partition, so it bypasses MIXED ROOT.
- **Test.** None: there is no candidate f_x yet.
- **Verdict: high upside, speculative.**

## Ranked shortlist

1. **The MS labelling as the induction invariant, plus the level-cut and star reading (F1, F2).**
   - It is proved where it applies and explains why the lock is exactly p = k−a.
   - **Next test:** at the keep-failing cex40 placements, find a BDL partition of X that changes the fewest B-points' bands relative to some BDL partition of B (a MaxSAT problem). Record how many points enter each band; Pascal numbers C(k−3,·) would point to a BTK structure.
2. **The linear-algebra / window-sum method** (ABS → Hwang–Kim, standard monomials).
   - It is choice-free and avoids BDL.
   - **Next step:** propose f_x recursively along the tree (Lemma DS), and test the rank of [f_x] against |X| on the corpus.
3. **SCD and bracketing as the shape of the multi-band exchange**, built from the de Bruijn–Tengbergen–Kruyswijk step.
   - The star-form failure on cex40 shows that the exchange must cross bands.
   - **Next test:** SAT-check BDL partitions of B that carry "chains", i.e. compatible injections X_j → X_{j+1} below the middle band, and see whether the BTK step rebuilds X at the lock.
