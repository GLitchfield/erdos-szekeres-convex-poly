# Area A: the Erdős–Szekeres literature and MIXED ROOT / BDL (2026-10-04)

Bibliographic data checked today on Crossref, arXiv or Dagstuhl unless marked [unverified]. "Read" means I read the
cited sections; otherwise I read only the abstract.

## 0. The main finding

Baek–Balko show that the window count T is exact for **split** k-gons in **every** 2-colouring of triples. A split
k-gon is a cap and a cup that share only their right endpoint, with sizes summing to k+2. The proof is a
Moshkovitz–Shapira injection v ↦ D(v) into down-sets of [a−2]×[u−2] whose bounding box r×s satisfies r+s ≤ k−1
(Lemmas 9–10). Here r = capEnd(v)−1 and s = cupEnd(v)−1, where capEnd(v) and cupEnd(v) are the longest cap and cup
ending at v.

The same hypothesis gives BDL with a canonical partition. I checked this by hand; it is not stated in the paper.
- **The rule.** Put v in band j(v) = max(k−a, cupEnd(v)−1).
- **Cups.** A cup inside band j has at most j+1 points.
- **Caps.** Let C = c₁…c_m be a cap inside band j, and let K be a (j+1)-cup ending at c₁, whose second-to-last point
  is x.
  - xc₁c₂ cannot be a cup, because then c₂ would have cupEnd ≥ j+2.
  - So xC is an (m+1)-cap ending at c_m, and c_m also ends a (j+1)-cup.
  - Since there is no split k-gon, m ≤ k−1−j.

In other words, BDL is canonical except at split-but-not-strong polygons.

**Sampled check (diagnostic, not exhaustive; /tmp/decomp_layer.py, not kept).** Of 289 random strong-free
decomposable colourings (n = 7–11, k = 5, 6), 150 had split witnesses and the layering broke in 56, all among those
150. The first break was a tight set, n = T(5,5,5) = 7. On 506 split-free random point sets it never broke.

**Decomposable sets produce split witnesses even without interleaving.** The project's 6-point set
(docs/plans/review-2026-09-03.md; experiment 0107) is {p₁} deep below and left of B. Its split 5-gon is a B-cap
with an A-point in front of it: Lemma DS's "A-point extends a B-cap", which is the move MIXED ROOT has to control.
Baek–Balko §2.3 say outright that their structure cannot force the two left endpoints to meet.

## 1. Papers

**A1. Erdős–Szekeres 1935**, *Compositio Math.* 2, 463–470 (verified on Numdam).
1. **What and how.** It proves the cups–caps bound C(a+u−4, a−2) by a Pascal step. Let E be the set of right
   endpoints of (u−1)-cups. The rest of the set has no (u−1)-cup. E has no (a−1)-cap, because at the shared point
   either the cup or the cap would extend.
2. **Use.** T satisfies T(a,u,k) = T(a−1,u,k−1) + T(a,u−1,k−1), and PSL is exactly this step lifted to strong sets.
   §0 is the same step iterated.
3. **Test.** On tight decomposable sets, set R = E and find the tree nodes where strong(R) or strong(rest)
   exceeds k−2.
4. **Verdict: unlikely**, since SAT already excludes canonical rules. It is the baseline to beat.

**A2. Moshkovitz–Shapira**, "Ramsey theory, integer partitions and a new proof of the Erdős–Szekeres theorem",
*Adv. Math.* 262 (2014) 1107–1129, arXiv:1206.4001.
1. **What and how.**
   - Each pair uv gets a label: the longest red path and the longest blue path ending with uv.
   - D(v) is the down-set generated by the labels of pairs ending at v. The map v ↦ D(v) is injective, because
     D(u,v) ∈ D(v)∖D(u).
   - Down-sets of an m×n grid correspond to line partitions n ≥ a₁ ≥ … ≥ a_m ≥ 0, and there are C(m+n, m) of them.
   - It extends to N₃(q,n) through (q−1)-dimensional partitions.
2. **Use.** The line partition of v is v's cap/cup trade-off curve: a per-point model in which T is structural (A3).
   - For decomposable sets the label must be two-sided. By Lemma DS a B-cap gains an A-point at either end, so what
     matters is where caps start relative to A, not only where they end.
3. **Test.** See A3(ii).
4. **Verdict: promising**, together with A3.

**A3. Baek–Balko**, "The Erdős–Szekeres conjecture revisited", SoCG 2025, LIPIcs 332, paper 13 (read §§2, 4, 5),
and *JCTA* 222 (2026) 106195 (verified on Crossref; I could not get the full text).
1. **What and how.**
   - Theorems 4 and 6: ES_split = C_split = 1+T for every colouring.
   - Theorem 7: C_weak(7) > 33.
   - Theorem 8: the decomposable-set bound; the SoCG version omits its proof.
   - **How Theorem 8 fails.** This comes from the project's audit of the JCTA text: the draft letter above and
     ErdosSzekeres/PublishedInductionSemantics.lean.
     - The induction is a scalar recursion over X = A << B.
     - For each child, an index is chosen maximally from that child's size, and the two parts are summed with a
       Pascal-window identity.
     - It breaks when an index falls below the window. The first case is (a,u,k,u′,a′) = (4,5,5,1,3), where
       1 + 7 = 8 > 7.
     - Sizes cannot see where A sits among B's caps, which is exactly the information MIXED ROOT needs.
2. **Use.**
   - The canonical layering of §0.
   - An injection count. D is injective into all down-sets, so Theorem 8 is equivalent to
     #{v : r+s ≥ k} ≤ #{unused window labels}, with equality on tight sets.
   - An injection from split witnesses into unused labels that is local in the tree would prove Theorem 8 without
     BDL.
3. **Tests.**
   - (i) Run the layering on all tight decomposable sets (exhaustive for n ≤ 9; tight sets at k = 6, 7).
     - Is every break an A-point plus a B-cap at a mixed root?
     - What is the minimum number of moved points needed to reach a valid BDL partition? Use SAT with a cardinality
       bound.
     - Do the moved points match MIXED ROOT's lifted set?
   - (ii) Tabulate D(v) for the witnesses and for the unused labels. Test whether "shift D(v) down by one in the cap
     coordinate" maps the witnesses injectively onto the unused labels.
4. **Verdict: promising.** It is the only exact proof of T, and the place where it fails is known exactly.

**A4. Baek**, "On the Erdős–Tuza–Valtr conjecture", *EJC* 124 (2025) 104085, arXiv:2206.04260 (read §§2–4).
1. **What and how.** It proves P(n,4,n): every set of C(n−1,2)+2 points contains a 4-cap or an n-gon. It holds for
   all abstract configurations, with weak gons.
   - A slope labelling s(xy) = c(xy)−1, where c is the longest cap starting with xy, satisfies s(xy) ≤ s(yz) ⇒ cup.
   - The α-statistic α_i(p) is the longest cup ending at p via an edge of label ≤ i. It injects into weakly
     increasing sequences, which refines Moshkovitz–Shapira.
   - The proof is an induction that finds two "interweaved laced (n−1)-cups" (p < q ≤ r < s); by Lemma 5.2, two of
     them force the gon.
   - Conjecture 5: a set of C(n−1,2)+d points contains d of them.
2. **Use.**
   - It is the only exact off-diagonal proof beyond cups–caps, and it proves a global existence of the same kind as
     BDL.
   - Interweaving mirrors "every max cap of B starts before A and ends after A".
   - Theorem 8 at a = 4 already follows, since T(4,k,k) = C(k−1,2)+1.
3. **Test.** For β = 3, is some valid band partition a function of each point's position in the (α,β)-plane, for some
   slope labelling?
   - Slope labellings are not unique, so this is an existential rule, and the SAT exclusion of canonical rules does
     not directly cover it.
   - Then try the α-vector for general a.
4. **Verdict: maybe.**

**A5. Erdős–Tuza–Valtr**, "Ramsey-remainder", *EJC* 17 (1996) 519–532 (verified on Crossref; not read), plus Valtr's
construction and Baek–Balko's blow-up (Lemma 14).
1. **What and how.**
   - The conjecture is N(a,u,k) = Σᵢ N(i, k+2−i) = T, and it is equivalent to ES.
   - P(n,a,b) ⇒ P(n,a′,b′) whenever a ≥ a′ and b ≥ b′ (as restated by Baek, Theorem 1.5).
   - The lower bound stacks cups–caps blocks, each deep below the next.
2. **Use.** The window sum is one block per band, and BDL says every decomposable extremal set is such a stack after
   reassignment. If gluing preserves decomposability and BDL, then the off-diagonal case reduces to the diagonal.
   That is bookkeeping, not a proof idea.
3. **Test.** Glue a tight decomposable set into Valtr's stack and run the BDL checker. Use Lemma 14 blow-ups as stress
   sets.
4. **Verdict: maybe** (tooling).

**A6. Balko–Valtr**, "A SAT attack on the Erdős–Szekeres conjecture", *EJC* 66 (2017) 13–23 (read the ENDM 49
abstract).
1. **What and how.**
   - It refutes Peters–Szekeres: abstract colourings give 17 > T(4,7,7) = 16, a 33-vertex colouring with no 7-gon,
     and Ĉ(8) > 64.
   - For pseudolinear colourings (signotopes), N(4,7,7) = 16 and N(4,8,8) = 22, which equal T.
2. **Use.** These are concrete sets on which every split repair must fail.
3. **Test.** Run the layering-defect and BDL checks on their published colourings (the ES_SAT addendum) and on the
   project's 14-point signotope, then compare with decomposable defects.
4. **Verdict: maybe** (diagnostic of what decomposability adds).

**A7–A12. Unlikely sources, briefly.**
- **A7. Szekeres–Peters**, *ANZIAM J.* 48 (2006) 151–164 (verified on Crossref).
  - **What:** ES(6) = 17, by exhaustive search over "signature functions", a superset of realizable configurations.
  - **Use:** certifying small cases only; there is no structural lemma.
- **A8. Suk**, *JAMS* 30 (2017) 1047–1053, arXiv:1604.08657 (read). **Holmsen–Mojarrad–Pach–Tardos**, *JEMS* 22
  (2020) 3981–3995, arXiv:1710.11415 (read §§1–2).
  - **What:**
    - A positive-fraction ES theorem gives a convex chain of large parts.
    - Each part is an antichain or a chain (Dilworth). A chain carries a transitive left-cap/right-cap colouring.
    - Adjacent parts glue: a left-cap in Q_{i−1} plus a right-cap in Q_i is convex (Observation 3.1).
    - Thresholds then climb (Q_i must contain an iK-left-cap).
    - HMPT carry this over to pseudo-configurations and get 2^{n+O(√(n log n))}.
  - **Use:** Observation 3.1 is Lemma DS, and the climbing is BDL's budget j+1 / k−1−j. But the parts are supplied by
    the geometry, nothing is chosen, and the method loses 2^{o(n)}.
  - **Test:** on caterpillar trees, does greedy climbing alone give BDL? If so, the difficulty lies in branching
    trees.
- **A9. Fox–Pach–Sudakov–Suk**, *PLMS* 105 (2012) 953–982, arXiv:1105.2097.
  - **What:** monotone paths in ordered hypergraphs. N₃(2,n) = C(2n−4, n−2)+1 for every colouring, and
    2^{(n/q)^{q−1}} ≤ N₃(q,n) ≤ 2^{n^{q−1} log n}.
  - **Use:** this is the abstract setting, where split polygons are exact and strong ones fail.
- **A10. Eliáš–Matoušek**, *Adv. Math.* 244 (2013) 1–15, arXiv:1111.3824.
  - **What:** kth-order monotonicity through transitive colourings of (k+1)-uniform hypergraphs, with bounds of order
    log^{(k−1)} N.
  - **Use:** none; there is no window.
- **A11. Generalized configurations.**
  - **What:**
    - Goodman–Pollack, *Congr. Numer.* 32 (1981) 383–394 [verified only through citations in HMPT and Balko–Valtr]:
      posed ES for pseudo-configurations.
    - Felsner–Weil, *DAM* 109 (2001) 67–94: signotopes are equivalent to swept pseudoline arrangements.
    - Bergold–Felsner–Scheucher, SoCG 2023; journal version in *Combinatorial Theory*, doi 10.5070/C65465668:
      signotope extension through prescribed crossings.
    - Scheucher, *CGT* 2(1) (2023): chirotope SAT with DRAT certificates.
  - **Use:** BDL fails in this generic setting (the 14-point counterexample), so it cannot carry the proof.
    Scheucher's DRAT pipeline is useful infrastructure for certifying MIXED ROOT instances.
- **A12. Morris–Soltan** (the brief's "Morris–Soifer"): *Bull. AMS* 37 (2000) 437–458, and the 2016 chapter in *Open
  Problems in Mathematics* (Springer), 351–375.
  - **What:** background surveys. Their abstracts show no window technique.

## 2. Ranked shortlist

1. **The split layering with Moshkovitz–Shapira labels (A3, A2, A1).** BDL is canonical away from
   split-but-not-strong polygons. In decomposable sets these come from an A-point extending a B-cap. Run test A3(i)
   first. If the moved points are MIXED ROOT's lifted A-points, keep-or-lift becomes "repair the split defect", with
   the trade-off curve as the measure. Test A3(ii) would bypass BDL altogether.
2. **Baek's α-statistic and interweaved laced cups (A4).** It is an exact induction proving a global existence, and
   its labelling is not canonical, so it escapes the SAT barrier on canonical rules. Test it at a = 4 first.
3. **The Balko–Valtr counterexamples and Erdős–Tuza–Valtr / Valtr gluing (A6, A5).** Cheap diagnostics: what
   decomposability rules out, and whether the off-diagonal case reduces to the diagonal.
