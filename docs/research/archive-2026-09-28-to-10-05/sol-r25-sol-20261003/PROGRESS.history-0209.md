## Current mathematical idea

Write `s = k-1`. A band `j` has cup limit `j+1` and cap limit `s-j`.
At a root `A << B`, let `p = cup(A)` and `t = cap(B)`; Lemma DS gives `p+t <= s`.
The nonclassic children's intrinsic band ranges are disjoint, so ordinary composition
works. The difficulty is fitting a classic child into the parent's narrower range when
the sibling retains the parent's extreme chain number. The tight nested case has
`cap(B)=cap(X)=beta` and `p=s-beta`.

The exact sufficient missing statement is **BRL+**, section 33 of
`BDL-OBSTRUCTION.md`: given an existing BDL partition of a decomposable set with
positive bottom, at least three bands and an open maximum-cap core `(L*,R*)`, rearrange it into
a valid partition whose bottom band lies entirely at or before L*, or entirely
at or after R*. Only one of these choices is required. BRL+ implies BDL by the
written size induction and duality. BRL+ remains unproved. Direct positional
induction now has several uniform constructions (sections16,19,20): cap gain2
leaves two separated free bands; at gain1 an interior lower anchor forces the
upper maximum caps to surround it; at gain0 lower interior points can enter the
next band because they cannot be endpoints of a parent maximum cap.

The residual geometry is split exterior lower anchors, a requested orientation
when only one exterior exists, and compatible bottom/top choices in tight dual
absorption. Candidate Q asks for a simultaneous core-side bottom/top partition
only with >=3 bands, the exact domain of that dual request. Q remains unproved.
Local one-point and lowest-two-band exchanges are refuted, so an exchange proof
must permit global simultaneous changes. No complete positional invariant is known.

The stronger low-two-band cup rule is false, as is simultaneous control of the
lowest-two cup number and highest-two cap number. Rigid adjacent-band union bounds
therefore cannot supply this induction. Different valid partitions must be compared.
