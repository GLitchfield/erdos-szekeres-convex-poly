## 4. Exact joint keep/lift criterion for a general sibling

For |A|>=2, p>=2. A second point of A generally cannot be inserted as
a lower singleton of B plus earlier A-points: a triple with its middle
point in earlier A has Cup in the original root but Cap in that purported
singleton split. The one-point criterion therefore cannot simply be iterated.

Fix one valid child map, put C=B_p, D=B_(p+1), and partition A into
kept K and lifted L. All K goes to bottom and all L to second. The
combined map is valid exactly when BOTH of the following hold:

    KEEP: c(K intersect open span(C))<=p-1.

    LIFT: d(L)<=beta-1;
          d(D_<maxL)<=beta-2;
          d(D_>minL)<=beta-2;
          if |L|>=2, d(D intersect open span(L))<=beta-3.

For empty K or L the corresponding conditions are vacuous. Pure bottom
cups already fit and c(K)<=p. A mixed cup has a K-cup and at most two
C endpoints. One C endpoint gives size<=p+1. Two give a forbidden
p+2 cup precisely when a p-cup of K lies between two C points. If such
a cup lies in span(C), the extreme C points bracket it; conversely
every forbidden cup has this form. Bottom caps fit by containment in X.

Second cups fit because c(L)<=p and mixed cups have size<=p+2. Pure
D caps already fit its beta-1 limit; pure L caps give the first LIFT
condition. A mixed cap has one L endpoint with a D cap on its side,
or two L endpoints bracketing a D cap. The widest sides occur at minL
and maxL, and the widest span is between those extrema. They give
exactly the last three inequalities. The extrema construct every
potential overflow, so the conditions are necessary as well as sufficient.
Other bands are unchanged. This is a complete paper equivalence under DS,
not an existence proof or an assumption that every parent has this form.

The new information beyond a singleton is therefore exact: an interval
cup rank for K, a pure cap rank for L, and one two-endpoint interval cap
rank for D. Checking individual points of A in the same cell loses both
interval conditions. Even all one-point tests on a common child map do
not imply joint validity.

If C lies entirely left of minA or entirely right of maxA, KEEP holds
with K=A, L empty. This yields a sufficient signed one-point positioning
question about B at an A extremum. An unqualified choice of either side
at just minA is insufficient: its right branch can still straddle a
maximum cup of A. A positioning premise that supplies the appropriate
side is a separate unproved statement for p>=2. Applying singleton
absorption with the SAME p>=2 makes stay automatic (a singleton adds at
most a3-cup), so it supplies no such positioning information.

## 10. Cored strong=cap sets have an outer cap transversal (paper proof)

Let B be decomposable, d(B)=beta>=2, sigma(B)<=beta. Fix an ordered
interval [ell,r], ell<=r, strictly inside EVERY maximum beta-cap:

    first(D)<ell<=r<last(D) for every beta-cap D of B.

There exists a nonempty whole cap F wholly before ell OR wholly after r
meeting every maximum beta-cap. Automatically |F|<=beta-1: a beta-cap
wholly on one side would contradict the interval hypothesis. This is
an either-side conclusion, not a prescribed-side assertion. Dedup searches
`cored cap transversal strong number equals cap` and
`maximum cap core strong equals cap one sided transversal` found no result.

Proof by size induction, using any decomposition B=L<<U. Write
q=c(L),t=d(U). The accepted DS join gives q+t<=beta. Both are positive,
so t<beta: no maximum beta-cap is pure U.

- If q=1, L is a singleton {a}. Since beta>=2, every maximum beta-cap
  meets U and contains a as ONE of its two extreme points. Every such cap
  spans [ell,r], hence a<ell or a>r. Set F={a}; it meets them all.
- If q=2, L is a whole cap. Also t<=beta-2. A beta-cap meeting U needs
  BOTH L endpoints, because one endpoint gives at most t+1<=beta-1.
  A pure L beta-cap has its two extrema in L as well. Thus EVERY maximum
  beta-cap has first and last in L. Set F={x in L:x<ell}. Its members form
  a cap, it is nonempty, and it meets every maximum cap at its first point.
- If q>=3, t<=beta-3, so every mixed cap has at most t+2<=beta-1 points.
  All maximum beta-caps are pure L. Therefore d(L)=beta, sigma(L)<=beta,
  and the same interval hypothesis holds on L. The size IH supplies F.

These exhaust every nontrivial root; beta>=2 excludes a singleton B.
This completes the induction. The argument uses decomposability essentially
through the DS join and the one/two-endpoint cap form. It does NOT claim
that all maximum caps share a vertex: F may have beta-1 points. R25paper36's
empty common-vertex intersection is consistent with this cap transversal.

The same induction gives a sharper interpretation: at least ONE of the
ENTIRE first-endpoint or last-endpoint families of maximum beta-caps is
a whole cap. In q1 every maximum cap has a as the same-side extreme,
so one endpoint family is {a}. In q2 both families are subsets of the
whole cap L. In q>=3 the maximum-cap family is unchanged in L, so the
IH applies to the actual endpoint families. These families are already
outside the interval and hit every maximum cap. Thus the transversal
can be chosen as a complete endpoint family. This is still an either-side
statement. It does not bound both endpoint families or assert a corresponding
statement at strong=beta+1.
