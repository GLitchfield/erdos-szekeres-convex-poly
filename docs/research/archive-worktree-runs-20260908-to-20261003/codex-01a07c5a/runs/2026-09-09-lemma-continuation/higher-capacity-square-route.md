# Notes toward the next quadratic marker bound

These are research notes, not checked Lean statements. The immediate formal sequence remains run-group minimum-three decomposition, inactive pruning, active boundary pair union, then chain-one straddling geometry. All drafts/search records are in this run directory.

The proposed central theorem has chosen global at most five, an actual chosen marker, all source children active with chosen capacity at least three (therefore exactly three), and uniform opposite pure traces L,R. Its target is the square of the common opposite rank width. The broader youngest-marker triple-face potential would use this central theorem plus the active boundary pair-union bound for ordinary anchored groups.

Finite evidence:

- uniform-square cut probe: 2,821 independent maximum-pair comparisons across 57 source configurations, 60 timeouts, no independent or coupled failure. Sources included arbitrary kept sets, unindexed markers and outside opposite-axis history; this is not exhaustive.
- two-child probe: all 46 examples attain width squared, so the proposed bound is sharp in that model family.

For source capacity exactly three and chosen global five, chosen pure chain extension is at most one. If source children straddle the chosen marker, the chain-one geometry forces every actual chosen marker onto one level between the two witnesses. The drafted general geometry theorem removes the old global-four restriction by changing only auxiliary globals/k, which do not affect marker geometry. A source with children only on one side instead has a boundary pair charge, at most choose(width+1,2), below the square.

The old uniform rank proof splits cuts into unit-own and nonunit-own cases. At the next degree, a plausible split is whether the chosen part has an active child of chosen capacity at least three. If none does, all chosen-part capacities are at most two, suggesting a linear rank potential across the regions before, between and after the new chosen markers. If such a child remains, two-level geometry forces it to an exterior and should permit a quadratic-plus-linear charge paired with a boundary companion estimate. This still needs actual proofs; merely replacing constants in the old proof is not valid.

A potentially useful normalization prerequisite: an indexed chosen level N has actual markers x<y, all children and all younger chosen marker positions lie strictly between x and y, and N is oldest on its chosen axis. Erase N while capping its global at min(global-2,k-maximumAt N). Both greedy directions can extend to the two old markers, giving two pure extensions. The old maximum term is maximumAt N plus the remaining younger pure extension sum. This should be the two-boundary analogue of ctx_boundary_marker_erase_normalize, retaining older opposite history. Search before drafting. It may bound the middle region of a chosen part with two chosen levels by the linear rank potential after normalization. The existing one-boundary theorem only subtracts one and cannot justify this step by itself.

Illustrative two-level shapes (tentative calculations, not yet checked separately): for chosen global five and opposite width w, S E1 E2 E1 S appears to have bound 2w-1, whereas E1 S E2 S E1 appears to have bound w. The latter is bracketed by two markers on the same youngest level; a two-boundary normalization would explain the extra reduction. These need explicit finite verification or formal proof before using them.

Do not infer the full cubic potential or parent additivity from this route. Both ancestor capacities at least four remain unresolved.

A further exploratory normalization may be useful for exterior regions in two-level cuts: an indexed chosen marker x at level N separates all children and all younger chosen positions (on the near side) from all older chosen positions (on the far side). Erase only N and reduce the chosen global G by one, preserving k and all other history. Unlike the existing suffix/oldest normalization, this inserts N between younger and older subsequences of an arbitrary pure greedy chain. Younger folds stay near x; a greedy step at N lands no farther than x; older steps have the same first selected position from any start on the near side. Thus one extra pure step should be available, while generic erasure already weakens join constraints. Correction at22:44 UTC: the earlier phrase “merged pivot group has a uniform opposite-rank interval” is too strong for individual children. In the checked endpoint coloring, left members have lower endpoint lo(x) and upper endpoint at most hi(x); right members have upper endpoint hi(x) and lower endpoint at least lo(x). The intervals may be nested and unequal. Merge witnesses make their UNION exactly Icc(lo(x),hi(x)). Thus an all-children-uniform-traces square theorem needs an additional nesting argument as well as mixed-capacity coverage before implying the full face theorem.

A precise conditional reduction can instead assume an anchored-square theorem for contexts where every active child left of the chosen marker has the marker's lower opposite-rank endpoint and every active child right of it has its upper endpoint. Its conclusion is the square of the active rank-union cardinality. This admits the nested intervals and mixed capacities actually produced by the central group. The planned reduction proves the full triple-face estimate FROM that explicit unproved hypothesis; it must not be reported as an unconditional context square/face bound.
