# Erdős–Szekeres project review (3 September 2026)

What a month of Lean, SAT, and agent-driven research on `ES(n) = 2^(n-2)+1` has actually established, where it stands against the published literature, and where to push next.

**Read:** branch `codex/state-578-tree-reservoir` at `c74c737` (pushed 3 Sep), 72 commits ahead of a `main` frozen on 8 Aug; 292 hand-written Lean modules (4.5 MB) plus about 11,000 generated certificate modules; 129 wayfinder tickets, 106 experiment notes, the map and frontier portfolio.
**Not read:** the generated CNF/LRAT chunk modules and `scripts/`, `data/`, `certificates/` on the branch (only `main`'s copies); the JCTA text of Baek–Balko. The project was not built for this review.

## The short version

The repository is a large, unusually well-audited formal research base. It has a clean planar statement, a proved sweep/orientation model bridge, the full lower-bound construction for every n, exact small cases through n = 5, kernel-checked proofs of the Baek–Balko decomposable-set theorem at k = 5 and 6, and Lean reproductions of the Tóth–Valtr, Suk, and Holmsen–Mojarrad–Pach–Tardos upper bounds. Zero `sorry`, zero axioms, and a serious trust policy. That is real work and a real asset.

Measured against the conjecture, though, none of the load-bearing edges has moved. The project's own ledger says so: `D-UPPER`, `D-HALF`, `D-DECOMP`, and `D-NORMALIZE` are all open, no portfolio entry is "advancing", every one of the thirty tickets written between 29 August and 1 September reports net proof movement *neutral*, and the last forward movement on the ledger (the Fin13 half-support, moving `D-HALF(k=6)` from 13 to 15) dates from 26–27 August. Most of the last two weeks went into governance and tooling rather than mathematics.

Three things turned up in this review that are not in the project's own record:

- **ES(6) = 17 is already formalized in Lean 4.** The ITP 2024 empty-hexagon development by Subercaseaux, Nawrocki, Gallicchio, Codel, Carneiro and Heule proves `gon_6_theorem` (every 17 points in general position contain a convex hexagon) as a corollary. The repo's bibliography cites the paper but records the six-point theorem as unformalized; `FP-16` is suspended for lack of it, and `PredecessorHalfSubsetAt 6` is waiting on exactly this ceiling.
- **Extremal configurations are mostly not decomposable.** A sampling experiment on pentagon-free 8-point sets (the extremal size for k = 5) finds that about 79% of the distinct sweep colorings are not decomposable, and 74% admit no deep-below split at all. The "normalize to the decomposable class" route has to change the coloring essentially; it cannot rely on extremal sets already being in the class.
- **The decomposable theorem cannot be derived from the split-polygon theorem.** A six-vertex decomposable one-change coloring contains split 5-gons but no strong 5-set. So Baek–Balko's Theorem 8 needs its own induction, which is exactly where the repo found the `(4,5,5,1,3)` boundary gap, and the arithmetic obstruction reproduces independently. Whether the published proof actually has the gap is a question for the authors.

## What is established

### Destination-level theorems

| Result | Lean | Status |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Sharpness: `2^(n-2)` points with no convex n-gon, every n ≥ 3 | `classical_lower_sharpness` | proved, planar |
| Exact formula at n = 3, 4, 5 (ES(5) = 9 via an 84-variable, 2,016-clause LRAT certificate) | `erdosSzekeresFormulaAt_three/_four/_five` | proved, planar |
| Model bridge: general-position sets ⇄ sweep colorings, convex subsets ⇄ strong polygons | `exists_sweptPresentation`, `containsConvexSubset_iff_exists_isStrongPolygonOn` | proved |
| Exact formula at n = 6 | none (`StrongSixCardinalityCeiling` is a `def`) | open in repo; see finding 2 |
| Universal upper bound `SweepExactUpperAt k` | only `k = 3` | open |

### Reproductions of published upper bounds

These are formalization achievements, not new mathematics, and the README says so. They are also the most reusable things in the repository. `tothValtr_upper` gives `ForcesConvexPolygon n (choose (2n-5) (n-2) + 2)` for every n ≥ 3 (the 1998 bound; the 2005 paper has +1). `suk_explicit_upper` gives `2^(n + 64·s²·clog₂(3n) + 128)` with s ≈ n^(1/3) for n ≥ 65, a conservative version of Suk's `2^(n+O(n^{2/3} log n))`. `hmpt_explicit_upper` gives `2^(n + 32·√(n·clog₂ n) + 128)` for every n ≥ 3, the published `2^(n+O(√(n log n)))` order. All three are certificate-free. Formalizing HMPT end to end in a few days in mid-August was the single strongest stretch of the month.

### The decomposable class

The abstract class matches Baek–Balko's definition (parts may interleave in sweep order; crossing triples are colored by the side of the middle vertex), and `IsDecomposable.isSweepRealizable` proves that every abstract member is induced by a planar configuration, a clean and probably new lemma in that generality. On top of it: `decomposableExactUpperAt_five` and `_six`; the hereditary states `DecomposableStateUpper` at `(4,5,5)`, `(4,6,6)`, `(5,6,6)` and their duals (all by kernel-checked LRAT, the 16-vertex `(5,6,6)` bridge alone being 155,064 clauses replayed through 256 checkpoints), plus a certificate-free, elementary proof of `(4,8,8)`; the symbolic cup-block gates `cupBlocks34/45/56/67Force…` and the diagonal gate `diagonalCupBlocksForce4kk` through k = 9. The 12- and 16-vertex certificates are stated for arbitrary `OneChangeOn` colorings, so they are stronger than the decomposable statement. State `(5,7,8)` is reduced to two singleton-tail gates but remains open; `(5,6,7)` and `(4,5,8)`/`(5,5,8)` are used only as hypotheses.

### The predecessor-half route

`PredecessorHalfSubsetAt k` says every strong-k-free realizable coloring on n vertices has a strong-(k-1)-free subset of size at least n/2, and `sweepExactUpperAt_of_predecessorHalfSubset` turns it into the doubling recurrence. It is proved at k = 4 and 5, and at k = 6 for n ≤ 14 (`exists_predecessorHalfSubsetAtSix_of_card_le_fourteen`, composing an elementary bound, a Fin10 fan certificate, the Fin11 window transport, and the Fin13 certificate: a 1,332-variable, 12,585-clause dependency core replayed through 256 kernel checkpoints). The remaining fixed-six cases are n = 15 and 16, which a compiled transport reduces to a single Fin15 endpoint.

### Infrastructure and negative knowledge

Roughly 45 of the 292 modules are counterexamples or negative controls: refutations of rank/downset profiles as recursive states, of incoming-star spectra, of adjusted Pascal capacities, of a dozen state-578 shortcuts, of prime-deletion descent, of the Monge energy route. These are kernel-checked and durable, and they are why the project no longer rediscovers the same dead ends. They are also not theorems anyone outside the project will consume.

## Beyond the literature?

New in the mathematical sense: the observation that a literal scalar `(a,u,k)` recursion cannot prove the decomposable-set theorem (the 652 statically unclosed rows for 5 ≤ k ≤ 12 in the off-window census), together with kernel-checked proofs that the theorem nevertheless holds at the first such boundaries. If the JCTA proof really relies on that recursion, this is an erratum-level finding with a corrected proof for k ≤ 6, publishable as a short note. New as a conjecture: the predecessor-half statement itself, with finite evidence through n = 14 at k = 6. New as formalization: ES(5) = 9 from the planar statement, the three quantitative bounds, the decomposable realization lemma, and (per the README; the module was not in the snapshot) Ringel-nine nonrealizability. Everything else, including the very large structural-charging corpus, is search information about one proof architecture.

Against that, the destination has not moved: no uniform producer for `D-HALF`, no uniform induction for `D-DECOMP`, no normalization for `D-NORMALIZE`, and (until finding 2 is acted on) not even n = 6. The 14 August proof-distance assessment inside the repo says this well; three weeks later it is still accurate.

## The routes as the ledger has them

| Edge | Needs | Where it stands |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `D-HALF` | uniform `PredecessorHalfSubsetAt k`, k ≥ 6 | fixed six open at n = 15, 16; the Fin15 obstruction tree compiles but both raw branches lack a cross-exterior color argument; the direct Fin15 SAT instance (4,823 variables, 62,218 clauses) has never been decided because every run hit a self-imposed proof-size or time cap |
| `D-DECOMP` | uniform `DecomposableExactUpperAt k` | k = 5, 6 closed; first k = 7 obligation (state 567) reduced to two 22-point packing obligations that are UNSAT computationally (994 MiB and 336 MiB traces) but not in Lean |
| `D-NORMALIZE` | target-reflecting descent into the decomposable class | consumers compile; the Layer-F/prime-mutation producer family is suspended after a kernel-checked seven-point counterexample |
| `D-UPPER` | either of the two above | open |

One structural remark the ledger does not make. The doubling consumer invokes `PredecessorHalfSubsetAt k` only at n = 2^(k-2)+1, where the hypothesis "no strong k-set" is exactly the negation of ES(k). Given ES(k-1), the half-subset statement at that cardinality is *equivalent* to ES(k). So the finite cases the project proves at n ≤ 2^(k-2) never feed the consumer; they are evidence that a uniform mechanism might exist, which is valuable, but they are not steps of the destination proof.

## Findings

### Finding 1 · the Baek–Balko boundary

Baek–Balko prove two things: the split-polygon threshold `ES_split(k) = 2^(k-2)+1` for arbitrary ordered 3-uniform colorings (Theorems 3–4, via the down-set injection v ↦ D(v) and the count of down-sets with bounding box r × s, r + s ≤ k-1), and the same window sum as an exact bound for decomposable point sets (Theorem 8, proof omitted from the SoCG version). The numbers coincide, so the natural hope is that in a decomposable coloring "no convex k-gon" already implies "no split k-gon", which would make Theorem 8 a corollary of Lemma 10 with no induction at all. It does not.

Counterexample (checked by `split5gon_counterexample.py` against the repo's definitions of `HasDeepBelowSplit`, `IsStrongPolygonSet`, and the one-change law): six vertices in sweep order `c1 < u1 < u2 < w1 < w2 < v`, decomposition tree `{c1} | U`, `U = A | {u2}`, `A = {u1} | {w1,w2,v}`, `{w1,w2,v} = {w2} | {w1,v}`, realized by the integer points `(1,6), (2,44), (3,59), (4,53), (5,50), (6,50)`. It contains the 4-cap `c1 u1 u2 v` and the 3-cup `w1 w2 v` sharing the rightmost vertex (4 + 3 = k + 2 for k = 5), three further split 5-gons, and no strong 5-set. So split-polygon avoidance is strictly stronger than convex-polygon avoidance even inside the decomposable class.

Theorem 8 therefore rests on an induction over the decomposition, and the question is whether the published two-sum induction handles the off-window children. By hand at `(4,5,5)`: split P = A ⊔ B with A a single point and B a 7-point decomposable set with no 4-cap, 5-cup or 5-gon. Any recursion that only knows the children's scalar parameters bounds |P| by 1 + 7 = 8, but the truth is 7, because a single point deep below B extends every 3-cap of B on whichever side of it the cap lies, and the 3+4 cup-block lemma the repo proved symbolically (`cupBlocks34Force455`) is precisely what closes the gap. That is the content of `published_induction_boundary_regression`, and the off-window census says the same pattern recurs 652 times through k = 12. What Lean checks is arithmetic about the repository's transcription of the induction; whether the JCTA text really "retains the next classical cup–cap term and treats it as belonging to an empty interval" is a reading this review could not verify.

Strategic point: the project has spent most of August building a replacement invariant (endpoint profiles, rank down-sets, structural charging, transition-Hall, gap tokens, pivots, reservoirs) and has closed individual states but no uniform step. Two authors who know the intended argument are one email away, and one of them, Jineon Baek, formalizes in Lean himself.

### Finding 2 · ES(6) = 17

[EmptyHexagonLean](https://github.com/bsubercaseaux/EmptyHexagonLean) (Subercaseaux et al., ITP 2024) states and proves `gon_6_theorem : ∀ (pts : Finset Point), SetInGenPos pts → pts.card = 17 → HasConvexKGon 6 pts` as a corollary of the empty-hexagon development. Its trusted base differs from this project's: the CNF comes from a verified Lean encoder, the LRAT proof was checked on the fly by the verified checker `cake_lpr`, and unsatisfiability is then asserted as an axiom in Lean. The repo's literature review lists the paper as "out of scope" engineering, so the convex-hexagon corollary was missed.

Two ways to use it. The cheap way is to accept their axiom-backed CNF result under an explicit, separate trust label and bridge `HasConvexKGon`/`SetInGenPos` to `ForcesConvexPolygon 6 17`; that immediately yields `ErdosSzekeresFormulaAt 6` with the existing sharpness theorem, closes `FP-16`, and supplies `StrongSixCardinalityCeiling`. The way that fits this project's trust policy is to take their encoder's CNF (with their symmetry breaking, which is what makes the 17-point instance tractable) and replay the LRAT through the existing `lrat_checkpoint_reify` pipeline; the Fin13 and state-566 replays were larger. Either way, together with the Fin15 endpoint this would make `PredecessorHalfSubsetAt 6` an unconditional theorem via `predecessorHalfSubsetAt_six_of_cardinalityCeiling_of_middle`.

### Finding 3 · extremal sets and the decomposable class

The `D-DECOMP` + `D-NORMALIZE` route presumes that the decomposable class is close enough to the truth that arbitrary target-free colorings can be pushed into it. A basic check not in the repo: are the extremal k-gon-free sets themselves decomposable? `decomp_probe.py` samples random integer point sets, keeps the 8-point sets with no convex pentagon, computes their sweep colorings, and tests the repo's recursive `IsDecomposable` exactly (all partitions, interleaving allowed, checked against the crossing rule).

| Sample | distinct colorings | decomposable | not decomposable | no deep-below split at all |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 600,000 trials, box 1000 | 621 | 130 (21%) | 491 (79%) | 457 (74%) |
| 300,000 trials, box 100 | 380 | 89 (23%) | 291 (77%) | 278 (73%) |

The checker was validated on the four-point case (all four sweep colorings of a triangle-plus-interior-point are decomposable), on cups, and on explicit interleaved deep-below constructions. Random sampling is biased toward generic order types, so these are not proportions over the 77 pentagon-free order types, but the qualitative conclusion is robust: at the first non-trivial size, the extremal world is mostly outside the decomposable class, and three quarters of it has no deep-below split at the top level. `TargetReflectingDecomposableNormalizationAt` is therefore a statement about rewriting the coloring, not about recognizing structure that is already there.

### Finding 4 · a correction to a worry in the docs

The docs repeatedly warn that "abstract" reasoning is unsafe because Balko–Valtr refuted a stronger abstract formulation. That refutation is of the Peters–Szekeres conjecture for *arbitrary* red-blue colorings of triples, with k-gons defined as a red and a blue monotone path sharing endpoints. It says nothing about `OneChangeOn` colorings (rank-three signotopes), which is the tier every certificate in this repo lives at. Whether 33-point signotopes force a strong 7-set is open; Szekeres–Peters' 17-point computation was at that tier and succeeded.

## On the process

The governance apparatus is now larger than the mathematics it governs. Between 29 August and 1 September the project wrote thirty tickets (about 43,000 words): eighteen are mathematical in subject, but twelve of those are read-only static audits whose lanes ran seven to twelve minutes and produced no Lean and no computation; only five tickets wrote Lean source, checked by the language server rather than the project build (the guarded build timed out at 1800 s in a generated chunk before reaching the new files); two ran computation, and neither reached a verdict. The other twelve are policy reviews, empty slates, generator restarts, scheduler status, and a Claude Code version mismatch. The 100-call generator ceiling, hash-frozen packets, failure genomes with five design-memory fields each, and per-lane 900-second solver caps with 192 MiB proof allowances are, in combination, the main reason the one decidable finite question on the critical path (the Fin15 endpoint) is still undecided.

The mathematical concentration problem is also as diagnosed on 14 August: the structural-charging and state-578 families are 124 modules and about 2.5 MB of source, essentially all of it descending from one attempt to repair one induction, and about a fifth of it consists of refutations of the family's own proposals.

2. Import or replay ES(6) = 17 and close n = 6: bridge `gon_6_theorem`, state `erdosSzekeresFormulaAt_six`, and finish `PredecessorHalfSubsetAt 6` by deciding the Fin15 endpoint properly (unlogged solve first, then cube on strong-five atoms with generous per-leaf allowances, then replay). If the instance is satisfiable, test the model's realizability.
3. Retire the factory, scheduler, genome, and per-packet ceremony for a month and spend the freed attention on two theorem targets with parameters in them: the Baek–Balko decomposable theorem in Lean, and the next Erdős–Tuza–Valtr case beyond Baek's.
4. Publish the predecessor-half conjecture and try to kill it computationally at k = 7 on structured 30–40-point sets rather than at the critical cardinality where it is vacuous.
5. Test `D-NORMALIZE` on one real example before building any more of it: take a non-decomposable pentagon-free 8-set from the probe and try to write down a target-reflecting decomposable coloring on the same vertices by hand.
6. Keep the reviewer loop; drop the consensus theater.

## Caveats on this review

The generated certificate modules (about 11,000 files, including every `*Unsat`, `*Atom`, and semantic-segment declaration, and also `RingelNineNonrealizable.lean` and the `lrat_checkpoint_reify` command) were excluded from the snapshot, so the LRAT trust chain is assessed from the docs and the consumer modules rather than inspected. Three modules use `native_decide`; two are counterexample files outside the root import, the third (`CommonAnchorPermutationOrder`) is inside it but has no downstream users. The JCTA text of Baek–Balko was not read, so the claim that its proof has the boundary gap is the repository's, reproduced here only at the level of the arithmetic obstruction. The decomposability probe is random sampling, not an enumeration of order types.
